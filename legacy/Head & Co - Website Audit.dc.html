Head & Co. — Website Audit

Design & front-end audit

Head & Co. website

An assessment of the current concept site against the business as it actually operates, and what has to change before it is shown to the owner or put in front of guests.

Reviewed The head-body-soul-space repository (main), the Fresha profile, and the Google Maps listing
Date 3 August 2026
Status Unofficial concept, noindex, not yet shown to the business

Verdict

The craft is in good shape and the content is not. The design system is coherent, tokenised and disciplined — colours in oklch, no hard-coded values in components, a considered type scale, real accessibility and reduced-motion handling. That is the expensive half of a website and it is largely done.

The problem is that almost nothing on the site is true. The six services it sells do not exist on Fresha. The durations are invented, the prices are blank, the opening hours contradict the real ones, the four specialists are lettered A to D, the reviews are placeholder sentences, and the photography is stock interiors from somewhere else. A site with 15 sections and no verified facts reads as a template, and the owner of a business with 2,231 reviews will recognise that in about ten seconds.

There is also a strategic gap. Head & Co. already converts well on Fresha, so a website that only repeats Fresha adds nothing. The site's job is the part Fresha cannot do: explain what a head spa is to someone who has never had one, answer price and duration before the click, rank for “head spa Jeddah” under their own name rather than an aggregator's, and prove — with measurement — how many bookings it sent. None of those four are currently wired.

Short version

Do not add features. Replace invented content with verified content, cut the home page roughly in half, wire booking and measurement properly, and shoot the real space. That is the difference between a demo and something they will pay for.

What is working

  • The design system. A named brand scale (ricepaper, espresso, walnut, taupe, moss, sand) in oklch, mapped to semantic tokens, with utilities for the shell, eyebrow and display sizes. Components consume tokens rather than colours. This is better than most production sites.
  • Placeholder honesty. Every invented field carries a status: "placeholder" flag and a file-level comment saying it must be verified. That makes the cleanup mechanical instead of archaeological. Keep this habit.
  • Accessibility basics. Visible focus rings, aria-labels on icon buttons, screen-reader text announcing that booking opens a new tab, a full prefers-reduced-motion block, and logical properties so RTL mirrors correctly.
  • The right scaffolding, unwired. A UTM-tagged Fresha link builder and a trackBooking hook already exist. They just do not point anywhere yet.
  • Server resilience. The SSR entry normalises swallowed 500s into a real error page instead of leaking JSON. Unusual care for a site this size.

Fix before you pitch

1. Put the real menu on the site, with prices

Six curated services, real names, real durations, real prices, each deep-linking to its Fresha booking. Forty-one services is a wall; six is a menu. Price is the first question a guest has and the site currently refuses to answer it.

2. Correct the hours and drop the “provisional” language

Open every day, 2–11 PM. Hedging notices in the footer and page leads tell a prospective client the work is unfinished. Verified facts do not need disclaimers.

3. Name the team

Nine specialists with 4.7–4.9 ratings are a genuine asset, and being able to request one by name is a reason to book on the site rather than by walk-in. Use monogram placeholders until portraits are shot — never “Specialist A”.

4. Use the real reviews and delete the Press section

Quote three or four short reviews verbatim with initials and dates, at least one in Arabic, credited to Fresha. Four empty coverage slots advertise the absence of coverage; remove the section until there is some.

5. Cut the home page from fifteen sections to eight

Hero, proof, what a head spa is, the ritual, the menu with prices, the space, the team, reviews, visit, close. The ritual-finder quiz currently sits above the services it recommends — move it to its own page, or cut it: a quiz is a question asked of someone who has not yet been told what is for sale.

6. Choose one origin story

Their own words lead with hygge; Fresha lists them under massage; the site leads with Korea. Ask the owner which they want to own, then use it everywhere — the alternative is a brand that sounds borrowed from two places at once.

7. Book the photo shoot before the pitch, or say so in it

Stock interiors are the single biggest tell. Either mark them clearly as placeholders in the walkthrough and present a shoot list, or replace the hero with one of their own venue photos. Silence on this point is the version that costs you the deal.

Fix before launch

Area Issue and fix
Booking hand-off Every button lands on the Fresha venue page, so the guest re-picks the service they just chose. Deep-link per service, and per specialist on the team page. This is the highest-value hour of work on the list.
Measurement trackBooking pushes to a dataLayer that nothing reads — no analytics is installed. Install one lightweight tool, keep the UTM tags, and report booking clicks monthly. Without this you cannot prove the site earned anything.
Local SEO The structured data omits coordinates, opening hours, price range, telephone, images and rating — exactly the fields that earn a local result. Add them, add a sitemap, and remember the site is noindex until concept mode is switched off. Today an aggregator ranks for their name; that is the recoverable prize.
Arabic The Arabic dictionary covers navigation and buttons only — roughly thirty strings — so switching language leaves an English page with Arabic buttons. Direction is also set after hydration, so crawlers and first paint get LTR English. Translate the content properly on /ar paths with hreflang, or remove the toggle until it is real. In Jeddah, half-Arabic is worse than English-only.
Images Ten full-size JPEGs with no responsive sources; the 207 KB hero is the largest-paint element on a phone. Serve AVIF/WebP with srcset and size for mobile first — most traffic will arrive from Instagram.
Fonts Three families and eleven weights load on every page, including the Arabic face for English visitors. Load Arabic only on Arabic pages and trim to the weights actually used.
Dependencies Forty-six UI components plus a charting library, a carousel, a date picker, an OTP input and two toast libraries ship in a brochure site that charts nothing and has no forms. Delete what is unused before handover; it is install time, bundle weight and future confusion.
Contrast Taupe on ricepaper — used for every eyebrow, caption and footer line — sits near 3:1 and fails the 4.5:1 minimum for small text. Darken the token one step; the aesthetic survives.
Small gaps No skip link despite a #content target; the 404 and error pages are unstyled defaults; the cookie-preferences link points at an anchor rather than a preference control — build it or remove it.
Permission The brand name, handle and reviews are being used without agreement. The current posture — noindex, plus a visible notice — is the correct one. Get written approval before anything is indexed, and agree who owns the domain, the hosting and the code after the sale.

Later, if they buy

  • Gift cards and packages — Fresha already sells packages and the site never mentions them. Gifting is the strongest reason a non-guest visits a spa website.
  • The journal is six articles of generic advice. Two good pieces — what a head spa is, and how to choose between the 60- and 90-minute visit — would earn more search traffic than six thin ones, and cost less to maintain.
  • A first-visit page answering the practical questions: what to wear, whether hair gets wet, how long before you can leave, parking on King Abdulaziz Road.
  • A monthly one-page report of booking clicks by service. It is how a website stops being a cost and starts being renewable.
